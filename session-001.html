The Big Idea

What the Champions Network Is

The Champions Network is a growing network of individuals and groups who come together around one truth: God engages the world in two distinct ways.

Left-Hand Kingdom

To Bless & Preserve

God blesses the culture of this world in order to preserve it for now — using government, family, and society to keep the world ordered and safe. This is where civic life, moral law, and religious liberty belong.

Right-Hand Kingdom

To Save

God saves individuals from sin, death, and the devil through the saving Word of the Gospel. Jesus lived, died, and rose again on the third day to save people and take them to be with Him for eternity.

The Champions Network equips people to understand God's two-kingdom engagement and then to live it out — being faithful civic citizens as God's ambassadors. There is a network of people learning how to engage, and then engaging fellow citizens and governing authorities locally, at the state level, and finally at the federal level.

We can do so much more by networking together than we can do apart.

Everyone who signs up for the Champions Academy is called a Champion Apprentice — because, as in any trade or science, we learn before we do. From there, a 5-Stage Journey leads toward becoming everything you can be as a Champion Knight.

A Champions Chapter is a local group of Apprentices and Facilitators who meet — normally once a month — to learn together and practice engaging. You join the Academy through a subscription and an access code from your local facilitators.

No local chapter yet? LCRL's national staff will help you join a Special Online Chapter until one is founded near you.

The Educational Hub

Inside the Champions Academy

The Academy is the main educational hub for the Network. Almost everything you need is online; some sessions send you to a book or your local library. It's facilitated locally, in your Champions Chapter, through six kinds of learning.

Individual Learning Modules

Easy-to-follow modules you work through on your own, keeping pace with the other Apprentices in your chapter so discussion lands better.

Group Discussion Sessions

A once-a-month gathering to discuss what you learned, underscore the main points, and practice your conversation skills together.

Community Engagement Plans

We don't learn for learning's sake. Engagement Facilitators help you get involved in local civic affairs — slowly at first, then as God grows your confidence.

Book Discussion Groups

A Casual Reader and an Avid Reader list, organized by chapter Book Facilitators — often meeting at a local coffee house, which can turn out to be evangelistic too.

Prayer & Worship Opportunities

National Days of Prayer, election seasons, legislative sessions — a chapter Prayer Facilitator helps coordinate prayer, including going out to pray over your community.

Two-Kingdom Bible Study

As your chapter grows in knowledge and confidence, you help facilitate healthy two-kingdom Bible study resources for others in your congregation.

You access the Academy through a subscription, with an access code from your local facilitators. Remember — we are not just learning for learning's sake. We learn and grow so we can literally engage people in our own communities.

The Objective

Championing Liberty — For Others

Being a champion for religious liberty is not about conquering the world for Christ, as if launching a new season of the crusades. It is about championing religious liberty for others — even for those who are not religious or God-fearing.

This reflects the Founding Fathers — Madison and Jefferson — who held that freedom of conscience is a natural, unalienable right underpinning all other civil liberties.

"If the government can control what you believe, it can inevitably control what you say, write, and do."
The idea behind freedom of conscience

Champions are servant-minded, with a Christlike posture. Some of us are eager to charge ahead — like Peter, quick with a sword. But Jesus, reattaching the servant's ear, says, "No, not that way, Peter." So we take time to learn principles like Dynamic Differentiation, Reformation Restraint, and Vocational Respect — slowing down to see what God is already doing in His left-hand kingdom before we act.

Every Session

The Five-Fold Purpose

Every Champions Academy session works toward five purposes — each one a part of your time together.

Inspirational

Inspiring you to live out your calling as a champion for religious liberty.

Educational

Helping you understand, navigate, and depoliticize the issues of the day.

Prayerful

Guiding your learning into the Lord's calling for faithful, public living.

Social

Supporting one another — and others — in two-kingdom engagement.

Missional

Building an advocacy plan for your community, so everyone working small tasks together raises God's voice for the good of all.

Learning the Language

Champions Vocabulary

A few terms every Champion should know from this first session.

LCRLLutheran Center for Religious Liberty

Launched by the Lutheran Church—Missouri Synod in 2017. The Center provides education, advice, advocacy, and resources in the areas of life, marriage, and religious liberty — engaging in Washington, D.C. on behalf of our churches, schools, universities, and seminaries.

LCMSThe Lutheran Church—Missouri Synod

A conservative confessional Lutheran denomination established in 1847 by German immigrants, headquartered in St. Louis. Its teachings align with the historic Lutheran confessions in the Book of Concord. It is the second-largest Lutheran denomination in the U.S., with over 2 million baptized members.

Champions NetworkLCRL's two-kingdom network of citizens

LCRL is the LCMS's advocacy voice in Washington, D.C. A growing, two-kingdom-minded network of citizens magnifies the impact of the Church's advocacy — in the nation's capital and at state capitals around the country.

Learn It by Heart

Our Theme Passage

Begin learning the Champions Network's theme passage — 1 Peter 2:15–17 — by heart, one phrase at a time. Learn a phrase this week, add the next next week, and the third the week after. Say it over and over; then say it to your children, your parents, your neighbors.

1 Peter 2:15–17 (ESV)

"For this is the will of God, that by doing good you should put to silence the ignorance of foolish people. Live as people who are free, not using your freedom as a cover-up for evil, but living as servants of God. Honor everyone. Love the brotherhood. Fear God. Honor the emperor."
