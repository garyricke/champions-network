Mark put it plainly: "Right now Boulder people do not know that there will be an individual fee. So, it looks free." Boulder met for the first time last night; St. Stephen meets Wednesday. Pastor Jarvis has been told $100 a month and promised a 50% discount as a Subscription-Pilot Group. Nothing can be charged until the board approves and Stripe exists — but the message can go out now, so nobody is surprised later and the pastors have one sentence they can say out loud. This page is that message, and the three decisions behind it.

1 · The offer (recommendation)

Founding members: half price, for as long as they stay. State the real price plainly. Then make everyone who joins through a chapter during the pilot a Founding Member at 50% off, locked in for as long as their subscription stays active. It's the Memento model Mark described on Aug 21, it rewards the people who said yes before the thing was finished, it's honest about what comes later, and it costs nothing today.

Three decisions, with a default for each

A. What is the standard price — and is it per person, per month?
Mark told Jarvis $100/month. In May the gift example was "$1,000 covers about 20 subscribers" (~$50 a seat). And Memento — the model Mark keeps citing — is $60 a year for Founders, $90 a year after. Those three line up only if the "$100 a month" was a slip for a year. The comparables in §4 say self-paced Christian study runs $70–$180 a year; only a heavily facilitated 10-month cohort (Colson Fellows) commands $900. Default: $99 a year, or $10 a month, per person — and $49 a year for Founding Members, which is the 50% Mark already promised, sits right on Memento's founder rate, and makes a $1,000 gift exactly 20 founding seats.
B. Does St. Stephen get the same pilot rate as Boulder?
Default: yes. Same offer, same words, both chapters. Two different deals in the first month would be a story that follows the Academy around.
C. Who says it first — the pastor, or the printed piece?
Default: the pastor, then the piece. Mark (or Carly) sends each pastor the short note in §3 this week so they can say it in the room; the kit insert and the chapter pages follow so it's written down the same way everywhere.

Where the Academy sits. More than an app (a chapter that meets, a mission plan, a discussion leader) and less than Colson (no book-a-month, no 10-hour weeks). That is the $90–$180 band. $99 a year is inside it, above Memento so it doesn't look like the cheaper cousin, and still an easy yes.

Founding-member practice, from the membership-pricing literature

  • Discount: the usual guidance is 20–30% off the eventual price. Mark has already promised 50% — honor it; the point of a founding rate is "maximum momentum, not maximum revenue."
  • Lock it for life: founders who keep their original rate become the longest-staying members. Say "for as long as you stay subscribed" so a lapse and return is at the standard rate.
  • Cap it and date it: 20–50 founding spots is common, with a clear deadline. For us the natural boundary is "anyone who joins through Boulder or St. Stephen in the 2026–27 chapter year."
  • Offer annual and monthly, and make the annual saving visible — "$99 a year, or $10 a month" shows the two free months without making anyone do the math.
  • Say what the money is for. The one public criticism of Memento (from a CTSFW dean) was that the site never says who receives the fee or what it funds. One sentence — "your subscription funds the Academy's production and keeps it in LCRL's hands" — closes that.
