LCMS Accounting — Academy Subscription

Microsoft Teams · join from Sam Deitte’s calendar invite (Aug 11)

Mark has been working this thread since June 5. Today is the first time the accounting people, Mark, Carly and Gary are all in the same room. The website side is not the blocker — banking and ownership are.

The one outcome to leave with A named legal entity for the merchant account, a named bank account for payouts, and a named person who reconciles it. With those three, the subscription can be live in days. Without them, nothing we build can accept a dollar.

Their two open action items LCMS owns

Sam’s July 31 update was encouraging: LCRL already has a US Bank account, and at low volume the tracking may be simple. Jim floated a new Org Code for Champions Academy. This may be smaller than anyone feared.

  • Determine how funds flow into an LCMS bank account
  • Determine how it’s properly accounted for on LCRL books and tracked

Questions to get answered Gary asks

  • Whose name goes on the merchant account — LCMS or LCRL?
    That decides the EIN, the onboarding documents, and who signs. Stripe asks on day one.
  • Which account receives payouts, and who can hand over the numbers?
    The US Bank account Sam found, or a new one — from a person authorised to give it, not forwarded through email.
  • Gross or net on the books?
    Stripe deposits net of fees (roughly 2.9% + 30¢). Accounting usually wants gross revenue with the fee booked as an expense. Whoever reconciles needs to know before the first deposit, not after.
  • Two revenue streams — do they need two codes?
    Subscriptions and Mark’s “Donate Now” are different animals: one is earned revenue, one is a gift and possibly restricted.
  • Who receipts the donations?
    Does the LCMS donor system need these gift records for year-end acknowledgment, or does Champions handle it separately?
  • Refunds and cancellations — whose call?
    Someone needs the authority to refund, and a one-line policy for the subscribe page.
  • Does LCMS IT need to sign off?
    Sam raised IT back in July. Worth clearing now — a security review shouldn’t appear after we’ve built.
  • How soon could we take a first payment?
    Even a pilot with a handful of subscribers proves the plumbing. Ask for a target date and who owns the next step.
What Mark has already told them — settled, don’t re-open

Mark answered Jim’s five setup questions on June 10:

  • Site: champions-network.com, with pages also linked from LCRLfreedom.org.
  • Processor: to be chosen with Gary — Mark has already floated Stripe to Sam.
  • Payment types: credit card and bank/checking, plus a Donate Now path for larger gifts.
  • Periods: monthly and annual.
  • Promotion: weekend workshops, Sunday bulletins, marketing videos, the weekly email, and the new Champion Chapters.
If they ask “what does the website side actually need?”

Short answer: very little, and quickly. Stripe handles card data, recurring billing, the subscriber portal and the receipting hooks. What we need is the entity, the bank account, and a person to onboard with. The Academy pages already exist — the subscription is a gate in front of content that’s built.

  • Card data never touches our servers — Stripe-hosted checkout, so PCI scope stays near zero.
  • Subscribers manage their own cards and cancellations in Stripe’s portal, so Carly isn’t doing billing support.
  • Every transaction exports clean for accounting; a monthly report can be automated.
Tone note This is their first look at us. Lead with “we’ll fit your process” rather than “here’s the tool we picked.” Stripe is the recommendation, not the premise.

The Advocate Weekly sample PDF

Vol. 2 · No. 21 as a clean five-page PDF, ready to attach or link. Short link: champions-network.com/advocate-sample

Why it isn’t just the page we already had

The version in the admin menu was built to pitch us on the idea — it opens with “What if the weekly digest built itself?”, walks through a four-step AI process, and carries a note saying the summaries were condensed by AI.

That framing works against the ask. The people receiving this are deciding whether to let the Advocate reprint their reporting. What reassures them is seeing how their story is credited and linked back — not reading about how the issue gets assembled. All of the internal framing is stripped out of the PDF.
What a source sees instead
  • The issue exactly as a subscriber receives it — masthead, both stories, both “Let Us Pray…” blocks, subscribe and workshop sections.
  • A visible credit line on every story naming the source and date, so they can see the attribution they’d be getting.
  • A short About this sample page at the end stating the policy plainly: every item is a brief summary pointing back to the original reporting, and where a publisher gives us a preferred credit line we use their wording exactly, the same way every issue.
One honest note in there

The two photographs are illustrative placeholders, and the PDF says so rather than leaving anyone to assume otherwise. Published issues use the publisher’s own licensed image where permission is granted, or no image. Saying that up front turns an awkward detail into part of the answer they wanted anyway.

Sending as @champions-network.com

Mail addressed to @champions-network.com already lands in your normal inbox — that part is running. This is the other half: making your replies go out under that address instead of your personal one. Roughly ten minutes, once.

Carly — Gmail
  1. Turn on 2-Step Verification Google account → Security → 2-Step Verification. Google won’t issue the password you need in step 2 without it. If it’s already on, skip ahead.
  2. Create an App Password Same Security page → App passwords. Name it Champions Network. Google shows a 16-character password once — copy it somewhere before closing the window. This is not your normal Google password, and it only works for this one purpose.
  3. Add the address in Gmail Gmail → gear icon → See all settings → Accounts and Import → Send mail as → Add another email address. Enter your name and Carly@champions-network.com, and leave “Treat as an alias” ticked.
  4. Enter these when it asks for a mail server This is the only fiddly screen. Everything on it:
    SMTP Server
    smtp.gmail.com
    Port
    465
    Username
    your full Gmail address
    Password
    the 16-character App Password from step 2
    Connection
    Secured connection using SSL
  5. Confirm the code Google emails a confirmation code to Carly@champions-network.com, which forwards straight into your normal inbox a few seconds later. Paste the code in and you’re verified.
  6. Make it the default, and set replies Back on Accounts and Import, click make default next to the champions-network.com address. Just below, choose “Reply from the same address the message was sent to.” That second setting is the one people forget — without it, replies to Champions mail still go out from the Gmail address.
Worth knowing: the message still travels through Google’s servers, so a few recipients on Outlook may see a small “via gmail.com” note beside your name. Everything else — the From address, replies, your signature — reads as Champions Network. If that note ever becomes a problem, there’s a paid fix; it isn’t worth doing yet.
Shelly — same steps

Identical process once he accepts the invitation, using Shelly@champions-network.com and his own Gmail App Password. Nothing to do until he’s accepted.

Mark — this is the catch
Outlook can’t do this. Microsoft only lets you send from an address that lives on your own mail system, so Mark.Frith@lcrlfreedom.org cannot send as a champions-network.com address the way Gmail can. It isn’t a setting we’ve missed — Outlook doesn’t offer it.

Three honest options, in the order I’d suggest:

  • Keep using your LCRL address. You are LCRL — for most correspondence that’s the right signature, and it costs nothing.
  • Point Mark@champions-network.com at a Gmail account and follow Carly’s steps there. Best if you want Champions mail visibly separate from LCRL mail.
  • Ask LCMS IT to add champions-network.com to the LCRL mail system. The cleanest result and the slowest road — worth raising only if the Network keeps growing its own staff.

Tell me which you’d like and I’ll set up whatever’s needed on the domain side.
